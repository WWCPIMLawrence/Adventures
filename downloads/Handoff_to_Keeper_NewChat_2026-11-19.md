## 2A. The root problem and Cog's recommendation (decision needed)
**Problem (owner's words):** the knob must be unscrewed to install fragments, and the same anticlockwise twist takes the whole cover off.
**Owner's idea:** reverse the bayonet so the cover opens clockwise, so normal knob loosening tightens the cover.
**Cog's analysis (reasoning, untested):** the knob turns both ways, loosening anticlockwise and tightening clockwise, and both put torque on the cover. Any purely rotational release matches one of those two directions, so reversing only moves the failure from loosening to **tightening**, which happens after every fragment (four times per play-through). It could be less severe (lighter torque; the knob clamps the cover at the end) but that is a guess, it needs a reprint of the deck and cover, and it changes the player action. The reliable fix is a **second action the knob cannot produce**.
**Recommendation:** keep the **push-and-twist gate** (press the cover about 1 mm, then turn anticlockwise 28 degrees). The owner reported the gate **works flawlessly in the hand** (16 Nov). The only physical failure was that the printed PETG strips fell out of the hub when the cover was removed (cause: slot 0.07 mm wider per side than the foot, no vertical capture). **Fix only that**, with captured strips:
- Strip feet become T-shaped (dovetail) and slide radially into T-slots in the hub top, so they cannot pull straight out; the inner ring, fitted over the hub afterwards, closes the open end so they cannot slide out. Installed once at build.
- Needs **four new gate decks** (the three existing decks are latch decks) and six strips per instrument plus spares.
- **Not yet modelled or checked.** Open: print tolerance of the small T overhangs, clearance of the inner ring over the strips, strip life (beam-theory estimates only: about 12 N press for the 0.8 mm strip, 18 and 24 N for 0.9 and 1.0 mm), and the residual risk that pressing the knob hard while unscrewing releases the cover.
- **Proposed process:** Cog models it and checks the fit; **one version-marked test kit**; one test (knob test: zero and four fragments, 10 cycles each, plus a repeat while pressing the knob); **hard stop Sun 22 Nov**. If it fails: choose between a mirror-direction kit or a game-design workaround (a clue such as "hold the dial while unscrewing the knob"), and drop the gate.
- Other options are in the report: a rotation detent by shape (a redesigned latch; the old latch failed) or accepting a free-turning cover.
**Please decide:** (1) approve the captured-strip gate test kit with the 22 Nov stop, or choose another route; (2) state the true preparation deadline; (3) keep the gate-specific clue ("press the dial in and turn it back") unless the gate is retired.

## 3. What happened with the gate (physical, owner-reported)
- Push-and-turn feel was validated earlier. But the **printed PETG bow strips repeatedly fell out of the hub when the cover was removed** (two sets, 8 and then a similar set, photographed loose beside the hub). The centre also "falls out after one twist" on the prototype, and the owner cannot say which hub version it has. The knob test was never run.
- **Cause (CAD, read from source):** each strip foot sits in a slot 0.07 mm wider per side than the foot (`bow_fit`; Cog's comment called it a press fit, it is a clearance); nothing holds a foot down once the cover lifts. Stiffer strips (0.9, 1.0 mm, prepared in `concepts/gate_contingency/`) do not fix it; glue is not acceptable for repeated player handling; a snap-barb foot would strain PETG about 18 % (estimate). The only credible gate fix is a one-piece spring crown plus a new deck, unproven until printed. Hence Option 2.
- The old three-arm latch is **not a fallback** (it fails: knob loosening drags the cover, ring snags).
