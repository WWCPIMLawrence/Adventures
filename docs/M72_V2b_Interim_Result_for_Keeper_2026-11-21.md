# M-72 Surveyor's Plate V2b: interim physical result for Keeper (owner photos, 21 Nov 2026)
Owner-reported with two side-by-side photos (V2b black-inlay plate and the all-brass V2 plate, each on a Chronometer). Mechanical and fit results are **not yet reported**: the numbered test sheet is still open. CAD statements are predictions.

## What the photos show
- **The V2b print worked as designed.** The black lettering, the concentric rings and the framing arcs are black on a brass body, so the two parts were evidently imported as one object and the colour change at layers 1 and 2 came out.
- **Legibility is much better than all-brass V2.** "WREN & CO." and "SURVEYOR'S PLATE" are clearly readable in the V2b photo at arm's length; in the all-brass photo they are hard to read. The black lettering gives the contrast the engraving alone did not.
- **Edge quality.** The black lettering looks crisp. The thin rings and arcs look like single black lines, slightly wavy or uneven in places, and doubled where two rings run close together; at photo distance they read as fine instrument lines.
- **Windows and notches.** The windows are clean and the numeral and symbol glyphs sit whole inside them; the arrow window shows the double arrow; the rim notches are visible.
- **Fit.** The plate appears seated and level on both Chronometers.

## About the readings in the photos
The Chronometers are held rotated about 90 degrees (the fixed-arrow window is at the right). The three windows show **II/TREE, VI/SUN and VIII/KEY**. Working back, the **inner ring is at STAR** but the **outer ring is two clicks off (X at the fixed arrow, not XII)**, so this is **not** the XII/STAR calibration state and it is **not** the verified signature. It matches the CAD prediction for an outer ring two clicks off (the symbols stay TREE, SUN, KEY; the numerals shift by two). The IV/TREE, VIII/SUN, X/KEY reading at XII/STAR still has to be re-confirmed on the V2b plate; V1 and V2 were confirmed earlier.

## Still open (owner)
Reading at outer XII and inner STAR on V2b; fit and release on each production Chronometer (easy by hand, no tool, no ring disturbed); stability, cover operation, flatness; the print time. Four-unit production remains on hold; V1 and V2 are the fallbacks.
