# M-72 Surveyor's Plate V2: physical result so far (owner photos) and a proposed V2b (black-filled lettering), 20 Nov 2026
Owner-reported and from three owner photos; the owner has not yet filled in the numbered test sheet, so items marked "not reported" are open. CAD statements are predictions.

## What was printed
One V2 plate, brass PLA, **printed exactly as loaded (top face down, lettering against the plate), no changes**, per Keeper's approval. Print time not reported yet.

## Results against Keeper's six items
1. **Legibility.** **Weak at handling distance.** In the full-plate photo "WREN & CO." is very hard to read and "SURVEYOR'S PLATE" readable only with effort; in close-ups both can be read. This is the low contrast of engraved brass on brass that was flagged as risk 1. The concentric grooves show as wide stepped ledges, more visible than the text but less crisp than designed; the lettering looks fuzzy with rough floors, consistent with the bridging cost.
2. **Notch pattern.** Notches at 3 and 9 o'clock are visible and crisp on the rim; the 12 o'clock double notch was out of frame. Appearance of the full pattern: **not yet reported.**
3. **Seating and orientation.** **Not yet reported** in detail. The owner says that on some Chronometers the plate **"snaps" into place and is held tightly even upside down** (cup-to-screw-head tolerance varies between units; positive for retention, but may make it hard to lift off).
4. **Reading at XII/STAR.** The photos show **IV/TREE, VIII/SUN, X/KEY**, all whole inside their windows, and the arrow window shows the double arrow.
5. **Stability, ring clearance, cover operation:** **not yet reported.**
6. **Print defects:** the engraving floors look rough and the grooves widened; no cracks or weak spots are visible near the notches or windows in the photos.

## Owner's suggestion, and Cog's proposal for review: V2b (nothing printed, not approved)
The owner suggests changing the colour of the lettering and/or the engravings. **V2b** fills the engraving with **black** (existing filament): the engraving is exactly two layers (0.4 mm) at the bed, so the black is printed in layers 1 and 2 only, in the lettering, rings and rules, and the brass plate body continues from layer 3, the same colour-change technique already used for the tile digits and the back plate. Two files imported as ONE object: black inlay (0.34 cm3) and the brass plate (V2, unchanged).
- **Benefits (predicted):** much stronger contrast (see the preview: black on brass); the engraving floors become solid, so the bridging roughness disappears; the geometry, depth (0.4 mm), notches, windows and seating interface do not change. CAD checks: the two bodies fill the V1 plate exactly (no overfill, no gap).
- **Costs and risks:** colour changes in layers 1 and 2 (purge waste and some extra time; estimate not measured); fine hairlines may still blunt; it changes the finish from all-brass to brass with black detail. **Keeper's brief said brass finish and no additional materials; black filament is already in stock, but this is a finish change that needs Keeper's decision.** V2 as printed and V1 remain the fallbacks.
- Files in the pack: `DO_NOT_PRINT_UNTIL_APPROVED_V2b_part_1_BLACK_inlay.stl`, `..._part_2_BRASS_plate.stl`, two preview images.
